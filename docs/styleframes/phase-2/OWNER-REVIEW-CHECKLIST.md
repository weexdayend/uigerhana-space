## 1. Review the shared identity first

All concepts must preserve the approved content verbatim:

- **Identity:** Irvan Gerhana — Product Engineer
- **Brand line:** From pixel to production.
- **Headline:** “I build digital products from product logic to production.”
- **Introduction:** “I work across product thinking, interface design, and software engineering—bringing complex workflows into software that is clear to use and practical to operate.”
- **Primary CTA:** Explore selected work
- **Secondary CTA:** View résumé

A concept is not stronger if its visual metaphor makes the person, role, headline, or next action harder to understand.

## 3. Responsive review

Compare the two shortlisted concepts at both sizes:

- **390 × 844:** primary mobile counterparts for A and D.
- **360 × 800:** supplementary small-mobile stress tests for A and D.

Check that name, role, headline, introduction, both CTAs, visual idea, and next-section cue remain legible and ordered. The 360 px frames test tighter line wrapping and spacing; they do not establish real-device, browser zoom, or OS text-scaling coverage.

## 4. Five-second comprehension

For each concept, look away and then view it for about five seconds.

- Is it immediately clear who Irvan is?
- Is “Product Engineer” visible without having to decode the metaphor?
- Is it clear what he builds?
- Is the primary action easy to locate?
- Does the dominant visual support rather than compete with the message?

Record any hesitation or alternative interpretation. Do not reward complexity for its own sake.
